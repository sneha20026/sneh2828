Know More About Piles Disease


What are Piles?

Piles, also known as hemorrhoids, are swollen and enlarged veins in the lower rectum and anus. They develop when excessive pressure is placed on the veins in the anal region, causing them to become inflamed, swollen, and sometimes painful.

Piles are a common condition affecting people of all ages, especially adults who suffer from chronic constipation, prolonged sitting, or straining during bowel movements.

Types of Piles


1. Internal Piles


  • Located inside the rectum.

  • Usually painless because there are few pain-sensitive nerves in the rectum.

  • May cause bleeding during bowel movements.

  • Can protrude outside the anus in severe cases.

2. External Piles


  • Located under the skin around the anus.

  • Can cause pain, itching, swelling, and discomfort.

  • May form blood clots (thrombosed piles), leading to severe pain.

3. Prolapsed Piles


  • Internal piles that extend outside the anal opening.

  • May require manual repositioning or medical treatment.

4. Thrombosed Piles


  • Occur when a blood clot develops inside a hemorrhoid.

  • Cause intense pain, swelling, and inflammation.

Common Symptoms of Piles


Bleeding


  • Bright red blood during or after bowel movements.

  • Blood may be seen on toilet paper or in the toilet bowl.

Pain and Discomfort


  • Pain while sitting or passing stools.

  • Increased discomfort in severe cases.

Itching and Irritation


  • Persistent itching around the anus.

  • Skin irritation due to mucus discharge.

Swelling


  • Lump or swelling near the anus.

  • Tenderness in the affected area.

Causes of Piles


  • Chronic Constipation: Repeated straining during bowel movements increases pressure on rectal veins.

  • Chronic Diarrhea: Frequent bowel movements can irritate and inflame the anal region.

  • Low-Fiber Diet: A diet lacking fiber can lead to hard stools and constipation.

  • Prolonged Sitting: Sitting for long periods, especially on the toilet, increases pressure on anal veins.

  • Pregnancy: Growing pressure from the uterus and hormonal changes can contribute to piles.

  • Heavy Lifting: Regular lifting of heavy weights can strain the abdominal and rectal muscles.

  • Obesity: Excess body weight increases pressure on the pelvic and rectal veins.
